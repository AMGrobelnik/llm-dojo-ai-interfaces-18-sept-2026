# The author's anti-wall-of-text devices

## 0. Slide size

Slides are 16:9 (13.333 x 7.5 in). The institute template you place in
`template/` (see `template/README.md`) must be 16:9; never ship 4:3.

## 1. Recurring devices (instead of plain bullet lists)

1. Bold colour-coded lead phrase + short plain tail, same line, colon-separated.
2. Numbered "N. Imperative phrase" title + a bold level-1 section bullet + 3-5 telegraphic level-2 lines under it — used to structure a deck's whole section flow, not just single-topic slides.
3. Screenshots as evidence instead of describing behaviour.
4. One big diagram/flowchart carries the slide; text is captions only.
5. 2-column layout: bullets left, figure/diagram right.
6. Comparison/data tables are usually pasted as static images; a plain native `<a:tbl>` table is fine when the data is simple.
7. Section dividers: one big centred phrase, nothing else.
8. Punchy 3-line closers, large font, no sub-bullets.
9. Fix an institute + department logo in the corner of every content slide; omit it from title and divider slides.
10. State full name and institutional affiliation directly under the deck title on the title slide; skip a separate credits slide.
11. List the talk's sections as a short numbered list on an early slide, then reuse those exact phrases as later section-divider or content titles.
12. Pro/con verdicts use literal ✓/✗ glyphs (green/red), not just bold-coloured text.
13. Most content slides carry at least one image or screenshot.

## 2. Measured numbers

- **16:9 rule** (measured with python-pptx on two of the author's own 16:9 decks,
  `deck-a.pptx` and `deck-b.pptx`
  — both agree): title 33pt bold navy (top 0.4in, height 1.45in, unchanged from
  the 4:3 master default); body lvl1 24pt, lvl2 20pt (explicit run-level
  overrides of the 21/18pt master default); body box top 2.0in, height 4.7in,
  spanning from under the title to just above the logo row. A right-column
  side illustration should be scaled to fill the column height (not just fit
  its width) — widen the column (right ~48% of body width, not 40%) if a
  landscape image is width- rather than height-constrained.
  Never ship these 4:3 sizes on a 16:9 slide (21/18 is the *floor*, not the
  default — only drop to it, 1-2pt at a time, if a bullet still wraps after
  rewording).
- Colours: navy headers, red `C00000` = negative/bottleneck, theme `accent6` green = positive/fix, default black body.
- Bullets/slide: ~2 top-level + 3-5 sub (slide 5: 7 total). Words/bullet: lead phrase 2-4 words, tail 4-10 words.
- Comparison "tables" are often pasted images, not native `<a:tbl>` — check the deck's XML.
- Font-size scale still varies somewhat per deck; the pt-hierarchy pattern (title > lvl1 > lvl2 > lvl3) is the constant, the 16:9 rule above is the default starting point.

## 3. Five rules to restyle a bullets-only slide

1. Bold + colour-code the first 2-4 words of any verdict/problem bullet (red=bad, green=good), colon into a plain short tail.
2. Cap sub-bullets at ~10 words; beyond that, replace with a diagram, table, or screenshot.
3. Comparisons/matrices become a table or screenshot image, never nested bullets.
4. Give content slides a right-hand visual; keep bullets to the left third, never full-width text.
5. One idea per title; a "(2)/(3)" suffix means split the slide, not add more bullets.
6. One bullet = one line — the fit-up must never accept a line that wraps: measure every rendered line and fail the build (reword the line) rather than ship a wrapped bullet. Bullets are self-contained (readable without the speaker) but short enough to talk over, not read aloud.
7. Text as big as possible: Body text is already minimal (one line per bullet). On every slide, scale fonts UP until the layout is full, keeping hierarchy (title > level-1 > level-2 > level-3 > captions), never comically big.
8. Side picture stays ≥40% wide. If the longest line, not the height, limits the text size, drop to full-width text and put the picture bottom-right in the free corner space only if ≥1.6 in remain; otherwise no picture on that slide. Layout pick: the side-picture layout wins whenever it can still set level 1 at **28pt or more** — a slide with a picture and readable text beats one with bigger text and an empty right half — unless the below-text layout reaches the SAME level-1 size and gives a larger fitted picture, in which case the below-text layout wins. Only when the side-picture layout cannot reach 28pt does the larger level-1 size otherwise decide. A missing figure file leaves the slide text-only: no placeholder box, no reserved space; the figure is picked up automatically once the file exists.
9. Gutter: text and a picture never touch. Keep ≥0.3in clear horizontal gap between a text box's right edge and any picture — for a side picture (item 8, layout A) narrow the text column itself by that 0.3in. Measure every rendered line against **its own** box, not just the longest bullet: each paragraph is measured against the text column minus that paragraph's own indent (item 12), at that level's own pt size. A line too wide even at the floor size is a content problem: reword it first (rule 5's order), do not shrink the whole slide for it. The same ≥0.3in rule applies to a bottom-right picture (layout B) against the text block above/left of it. In layout A the picture takes whatever column width the text does not need at its chosen size, never trading text size for picture width.
10. Pictures anchor bottom-right and may use the footer band: every picture (layout A's side picture and layout B's corner picture) sits flush with the bottom-right corner of its region and may extend down into the bottom-right corner of the slide, over the right-hand footer logo and footer strip (which is otherwise protected) — down to 0.15in above the physical slide edge. Size a corner picture as the largest bottom-right rectangle *free of text*, not as the strip under the last line: for each candidate top edge (each rendered line's bottom, plus the body top) the usable width is the slide's body right edge minus the 0.3in gutter minus the right edge of the widest line whose vertical span reaches below that top — lines that end above the picture do not constrain it — and the height/width pair maximising the picture's area at its own aspect ratio wins. Use the measured line widths the fit-up already computed, never the text box width. The ≥1.6in minimum picture-leftover rule (item 8) and the "pick the layout giving the larger level-1 size" rule both still apply; this just gives layout B's picture more vertical room to qualify and to grow, so more slides end up with a picture than before. One proviso: the picture must cover the right-hand footer block *entirely* (a picture too narrow for that stops above the footer instead — half a hidden label looks broken — and is dropped if it then falls under the 1.6in minimum); it may freely cover the slide number's spot (item 11) since the number moves out of the way instead (item 15). The leftover is measured from the bottom of the last rendered line down to the picture's bottom limit. A second proviso, keeping the footer band tidy on the left: any picture whose bottom edge reaches into the footer band (below LOGO_TOP) is narrowed so its left edge never passes FOOTER_BAND_MIN_LEFT_EMU — the bottom-left logo (FOOTER_CLEAR_LEFT_EMU) plus the number box (SLIDE_NUM_W) plus a gutter, 3.04in on the institute template — so the left logo is never covered and the number's slide-left fallback slot (item 15) always exists; if narrowing that far then makes the picture too narrow to cover the right footer block entirely, it falls back to stopping at LOGO_TOP instead (full width allowed there, since it no longer competes for the footer-row number slot).
11. Slide numbers: every content slide (all but the title slide) shows its 1-based position in the final deck as small muted-grey text (~11-12pt) — see item 15 for exactly where it sits and how it steps out of a picture's way. Verify this programmatically after saving the .pptx (re-open it with python-pptx and assert each content slide's number text equals its index) as part of the build script, not just by eye.
12. No standalone headings inside the body: everything on a content slide's body is a bullet. A section line is a level-1 bullet (bold, colour-coded); its items are level-2 bullets; their details are level-3. Indentation must read at a glance — each level's text starts ≥0.5in further right than the previous one (`marL` step, with a negative `indent` so the glyph hangs in that gap) and carries its own bullet glyph (● / • / –). Font size steps down per level (level 1 ≥ level 2 ≥ level 3) inside the existing caps, and all three levels are fitted together by the "as big as possible" rule (item 7). Never render a heading as a separate full-width band above the columns: one mechanism, one indent table, agenda and demo slides included.
13. Flat multi-column list: a headline plus a long flat list of short, one-line items (no sub-bullets, no side picture) goes in up to 3 columns of UNEQUAL width — each column exactly as wide as its own longest item, never a shared equal share — laid out newspaper order (column 1 top to bottom, then column 2, ...) with ≥0.3in gutter between columns. Font size is the largest that fits both the available width and height, floored at 16pt; no item ever wraps to a second line. When the list doesn't fit even at that floor, split it into two slides, "<headline> (1/2)" and "<headline> (2/2)", rather than shrinking further or wrapping.
14. Pictures as big as the space allows: text is fitted first; every level-1 size from the fitted maximum down to 28 pt counts as big enough, and among those the layout and size with the largest picture win (ties to the larger text); the picture then fills the largest text-free rectangle (below the text, right of it, or both) at its own aspect ratio, ≥0.3 in from every text line and above the footer limit. Text inside an image must be readable from the back of the room, at least ~12 pt at the rendered size; if no layout can show it that big, regenerate the image with fewer, larger labels instead of shrinking it.
15. Slide number placement: the number takes the RIGHTMOST FREE SPOT on the footer row, right-aligned text in a 0.5in-wide box (two digits at 11pt need only ~0.2in), with 0.3in padding to everything there — template footer blocks and pictures alike. Its primary spot is (1) the true bottom-right corner (right edge 0.3in from the slide edge) only if the template leaves that corner empty — a template-geometry constant, False for the institute template, whose right-hand footer block (department label + logo) fills the corner — else (2) right-aligned just left of that footer block (right edge FOOTER_RIGHT_LEFT − 0.3in), on the same footer row as the template's bottom-left logo (top ~6.83in on this 16:9 template — measured once against the template and kept as a constant); a picture may still reach PIC_BOTTOM_LIMIT and cover the right footer block entirely (item 10) — the number moves instead of blocking it. Only a picture that actually enters the footer band (its bottom edge below LOGO_TOP) can block a footer-row slot at all; one that stops at or above LOGO_TOP never blocks the row, however small the vertical gap. When a picture that does enter the band intersects the primary box grown by 0.3in on every side, the number falls back in order: (3) bottom-centre (box centred on the slide's horizontal middle), if that box (also grown by 0.3in) is clear of the picture; (4) otherwise slide left along the footer row so the number's right edge ends 0.3in before the picture's left edge, never left of the footer clear-zone start (2.24in on this template, i.e. right of the bottom-left logo); (5) if even that is covered — the picture spans the whole footer row — keep the primary spot and add the number LAST in z-order, on top of every picture, and print a warning in the build report. The padding between the number and any picture is never less than 0.3in. Step (5) should never actually trigger: item 10's picture-sizing keeps any footer-band picture's left edge at or right of FOOTER_BAND_MIN_LEFT_EMU (the footer clear-zone start plus the 0.5in number box plus a 0.3in gutter, 3.04in on this template), narrowing the picture or dropping it back to LOGO_TOP rather than ever leaving no free slot for the number — step (5) remains only as a guarded fallback, never reached by any layout described here.
16. Picture variety: a picture belongs on most content slides, not necessarily every one — never place the exact same decorative image on two consecutive slides; only an information-carrying figure (a diagram or chart actually being discussed on both slides, passed as `key_figure=True`) may repeat there. When two consecutive slides would otherwise share a decorative picture, keep it on whichever slide it fits best (usually the first) and drop it from the other, which then falls back to the text-only layout (text fitted as large as possible, no picture forced in) — do not generate a replacement image just to fill the gap. The build script's report warns whenever two consecutive slides reference the same image path and neither is flagged `key_figure`.
17. Section titles name the theme: a slide title says what the slide is about, never a bare counter such as "Tips (2/4)" or "Part 3". When one topic spans several slides, each slide gets its own theme title ("Agentic CLIs: hooks"), and a counter is allowed only as a suffix after a theme, never as the whole title.
18. End on takeaways: the final slide is "Key takeaways" with at most three short level-1 bullets that restate decisions the audience should leave with, drawn from the deck's own content. No "Thank you" or "Questions?" slide; contact or URL lines, if any, go in one small line under the takeaways.
19. Progress marker on section openers (nice to have, not required): when a deck walks through an ordered sequence of stages (four interfaces, five pipeline steps), each section-opener slide shows the sequence as a vertical strip of native shapes in the picture column, all stages listed, the current one filled in the template accent colour with white text, the rest outlined in muted grey with grey text, small arrows between boxes, labels at 20pt or more, identical geometry on every opener. The strip counts as a key figure for item 16 and replaces that slide's decorative picture. If one opener already carries an information-carrying figure, that slide skips the strip. Try it whenever the deck has such a sequence; drop it when the sequence is not the deck's spine.
20. A multi-line title never leaves a lone short word (an article, preposition or conjunction such as "a", "the", "of", "and") dangling at the end of a line. Break a two-line title explicitly at its phrase boundary with a line break in the text, each line fitted on its own, instead of letting the text box wrap it: "The four AI interfaces: a practical guide" breaks after the colon, so the second line is "a practical guide", not "practical guide" with "a" stranded above. Implementation: `paragraph.add_line_break()` between the two runs, and the title font size is the minimum of the per-line fits.

## 4. Optional devices (used sometimes, not every deck)

- Reuse the same diagram asset verbatim across related decks when it fits.
- Render dense code/logic/prompt content as a dark-background monospace box, not bullet prose.
- Embed a full verbatim prompt or quote as one non-bulleted text block instead of paraphrasing into bullets.
- Bookend a long deck: repeat the same architecture-overview diagram near the start and again near the end.
- Show 3-4 small abstract thumbnails across the title slide top instead of one hero image.
