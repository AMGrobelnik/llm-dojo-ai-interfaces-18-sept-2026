**Shared-origin accounting.** arXiv is a per-work repository, so each arXiv row is its own
registrable origin (co-location on arxiv.org is not shared authorship). **S10 and S15 share the
origin `aclweb.org`** — they are the same conference's own pages, so they count as **one vote**,
not two; nothing load-bearing rests on their agreement. Other non-arXiv origins are single-source:
`aclrollingreview.org` (S1), `cljournal.org` (S9), `judge2026.github.io` (S25).

**Tier:** **L** = landscape / synthesis / critique / negative result / defining work;
**A** = official venue or publisher artifact (a submission policy or call stating the field's own
bar). **Peer-review status is stated per row.** arXiv rows are **preprints (not peer-reviewed)**
unless a venue is named (S2 → NeurIPS 2025 D&B; S3 → EMNLP 2023; S8 → ACL 2026 **Best Paper**;
S11 → TACL 2024 journal; S13 → EMNLP 2025; S14 → ICML 2026 Position Track; S16 → Findings of
EMNLP 2023; S17 → EACL 2026;
S18 → CoNLL 2023 BabyLM proceedings; S21 → ACL 2025; S23 → ACL 2026). The correction rule applies:
a critique outranks what it corrects. All quotes re-verified 2026-07-27 with the same extractor

## Priors omitted / compressed

**Dropped as universal** (the pipeline's generic research block already enforces these): report
baselines and ablations; do not train on test; state limitations; significance-test comparisons;
describe annotation procedures.

**Compressed to Ground rules** (a strong model already knows these; kept one line each because a
weaker, different, or future consumer might not): what surprisal, minimal pairs, BLiMP, FLORES+,
inter-annotator agreement, and the ACL Anthology / ARR pipeline are; that CL and NLP are used
near-interchangeably for the venue but not for the contribution type.

**Deliberately NOT re-taught:** how to build or fine-tune a language model; prompt engineering;
retrieval and agent architecture. These are NLP-engineering topics the consumer's live tools cover
better than a static map.

## Acceptance CQs

Standing regeneration acceptance test — a regenerated handbook must still let a reader answer these
from its text alone. Whatever a tool-equipped baseline already answers feeds Priors omitted.

1. A model fails a metalinguistic prompt about a construction. What may you conclude, and what not?
2. Where does grammaticality live more sharply — in string probability or in hidden states?
3. What happens to surprisal's fit to human reading times as a model trains past ~2B tokens, and does it hold for brain data?
4. Name the single most-cited weakness of the 445-benchmark review's corpus of NLP benchmarks.
5. Why is a catalogue count a poor measure of whether a language is resourced?
6. What does FLORES+ get wrong for genuinely low-resource evaluation?
7. What did the BabyLM challenge find about curriculum learning?
8. What are the three stances on LLMs' relevance to linguistic theory, and which explanatory status do current LLMs reach?
9. What does *Computational Linguistics* print as the bar for a squib?
10. Is peer-review quality in ACL-family venues measurably declining?
11. When should annotator disagreement be aggregated away?
12. Name three computational-linguistics threads that are already saturated as of 2026-07.
