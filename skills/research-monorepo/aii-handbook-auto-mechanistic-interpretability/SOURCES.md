# Sources — aii-handbook-auto-mechanistic-interpretability

**Shared-origin accounting.** arXiv is a per-work repository, so each arXiv row is its own
registrable origin (co-location on arxiv.org is not shared authorship — no one-vote grouping
applies among them). Non-arXiv origins are each single-source here: `transformer-circuits.pub`
(S11), `ai-frontiers.org` (S7), `greaterwrong.com` (S6), `darioamodei.com` (S15),
`mechinterpworkshop.com` (S14). Where two rows would share a registrable origin, corroboration
counts as **one vote**, not two.

**Tier:** **L** = landscape / synthesis / critique / negative result / defining work;
**A** = executable or official venue artifact (a call-for-papers stating the venue's own evidence
bar); **B** = incident / team narrative ("why we moved off X"); **D** = self-report.
**Peer-review status is stated per row.** arXiv rows are **preprints (not peer-reviewed)**
unless a venue is named (S5 → NeurIPS 2025 Spotlight; S10 → ICML 2025; S22 → BlackboxNLP 2025
proceedings). The correction rule applies: a critique outranks what it corrects. All quotes
re-verified 2026-07-27 with the same extractor `verify_quotes.py` uses.

## Priors omitted / compressed

**Dropped as universal** (the pipeline's generic research block already enforces these): report
baselines; ablate; match claims to evidence; state limitations; do not train on test.

**Compressed to Ground rules** (a strong model already knows these; kept one line each because a
weaker or future consumer might not): what a circuit / feature / probe / steering vector is;
superposition and the linear representation hypothesis as the working canon; activation patching
as the gold-standard causal metric; SAEs as sparse dictionary learning over activations;
transcoders and crosscoders as the attribution-graph and model-diffing primitives.

**Decomposition primitives are covered here** (2026-07-29 merge): absorption as a consequence of
the sparsity objective [S30], the AxBench raw-latent verdict [S31] and its rebuttal [S25], SAEBench's
proxy-to-downstream gap [S32], non-canonical units [S33], the crosscoder L1 artifact and its fix
[S34], seed dependence as basis ambiguity [S35], and what the flagship open fleet now ships [S36].
This is the SOLE interpretability handbook; there is no separate SAE deep-dive to consult.

## Acceptance CQs

Standing regeneration acceptance test — a regenerated handbook must still let a reader answer
these from its text alone. Whatever a tool-equipped baseline already answers feeds Priors omitted.

1. Is a discovered circuit a fixed property of the model? What does the variance evidence say?
2. Two papers report structurally different circuits for one task. What does that license you to conclude?
3. Why is an unrestricted alignment map fatal to a causal-abstraction claim?
4. What baseline invalidates a high auto-interpretability score?
5. On MIB, how do SAE features compare to raw neurons for causal variable localization?
6. What fraction of prompts do Anthropic's attribution graphs give satisfying insight on?
7. Name the two alternative evidence standards the ICML 2026 MI workshop will accept.
8. A CoT omits a hint that changed the answer. Is that proof of unfaithfulness?
9. What is the measured gap between internal decodability and output correction?
10. What does making a transformer's weights sparser cost, and where does the approach stop scaling?
11. Which is the dominant error source in attribution patching — local curvature or downstream non-linearity?
12. Name three interpretability threads that are already saturated as of 2026-07.
